\subsection{Feature engineering}
We represent each battle using 450 numerical features derived from the teams at
preview and, optionally, player ratings. Base statistics, types and the type
chart come from Pok\'emon Showdown data through poke-env~\cite{showdown,pokeenv}.
Features are expressed from player~1's perspective as differences between the
teams. The label $y=1$ indicates that player~1 won.

\paragraph{Feature groups.}
The 19 stat features compare the team mean, maximum and minimum of each of the
six base stats, plus mean base stat total. The 20 type features compare counts
of the 18 types, uncovered defensive weaknesses and the largest number of
members weak to any one type. Five matchup features compare super-effective
coverage, the number of favorable attacking pairs, defensive switch-in options,
mean log-type effectiveness and the number of pairs with higher base Speed.
We consider attacks matching the species' own types, and ability immunities
only when every possible ability grants the same immunity. The 402 species
features encode presence on player~1's team as $+1$, on player~2's as $-1$ and
on both or neither as zero. Cosmetic forms are merged, and only species
observed in at least 20 training battles receive a column. Three learned features
summarize historical species win rates, cross-team species-pair win rates and
within-team species co-occurrence. The last feature is the difference between
player ratings; it is excluded in the teams-only setting.

\paragraph{Leakage and augmentation.}
Learned win rates are estimated using five-fold out-of-fold calculations on
training battles: each row receives estimates fitted without its own fold.
Validation and test use estimates fitted on all training battles. To reduce
variance for rare species and pairs, a rate with $w$ wins in $n$ observations
is smoothed as $(w+50)/(n+100)$, corresponding to a prior centered at 50\%.
Unseen species receive a neutral historical win-rate estimate and can still be
represented by their known stats and types. We also augment training by
negating every feature and flipping the label ($y\mapsto 1-y$), representing
the same battle with players exchanged. This doubles 70{,}000 training battles
to 140{,}000 rows, while validation and test remain unchanged. This symmetry
balances training labels without introducing future information.
