PREDICTING WHO WINS A POKEMON BATTLE: RESULTS SUMMARY
COSC 4368 Final Project | final test run 2026-11-28
==========================================================================

Question: from the two six-Pokemon teams at team preview (Gen 9 OU), how well
can we predict who wins?

Data: 100,000 rated battles (both players >= 1300, within 100 points), split
by time. Train: 70k battles (Jun 2025 - Apr 2026). Val: 15k (to Jul 2026).
Test: 15k (to Oct 2026). Each battle has 450 features, all written as
player 1 minus player 2.

Every number below is on the TEST set, which was scored once. Brackets are
95% bootstrap intervals (1,000 resamples of the test battles).
Files: results/main_results.csv, results/baselines.csv.


1. RESULTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

                        TEAMS ONLY                     TEAMS + RATINGS
                        accuracy             log loss  accuracy             log loss
  Logistic regression   53.6% [52.9, 54.5]   0.6892    54.3% [53.4, 55.0]   0.6881
  Random forest         53.3% [52.5, 54.1]   0.6898    54.2% [53.4, 54.9]   0.6888
  Gradient boosting     53.8% [53.0, 54.6]   0.6891    53.7% [52.8, 54.5]   0.6884

  Baselines (test)      coin flip 49.7% (log loss 0.6931)

  Chosen settings (tuned on val): logistic C = 0.001; random forest
  max_depth = 20 (300 trees); gradient boosting max_depth 7 / 59 rounds
  (teams only) and 5 / 172 rounds (teams + ratings).


2. WHAT THE RESULTS SAY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

a) The teams alone predict the winner, but only a little.
   Every model beats a coin flip with room to spare: even the low end of
   every interval is above 52.5%. The models also beat every simple rule
   (best: 51.9%). But the edge is about 3.5-4 points over a coin flip.

b) The three models are tied.
   Each model was compared with logistic regression on the same resampled
   battles (paired bootstrap). Every difference interval contains 0, so
   this test set can't tell them apart. The simplest model, logistic
   regression, does as well as the tree models. Averaging all three models'
   probabilities doesn't help either (log loss -0.0003, interval
   [-0.0008, +0.0002]).

c) Knowing the ratings helps, a little, and the gain is real.
   Paired check, same model with vs. without ratings:
     logistic        +0.6 points accuracy [+0.1, +1.2]   log loss -0.0012 [-0.0017, -0.0006]
     random forest   +0.8 points          [+0.2, +1.4]   log loss -0.0010 [-0.0014, -0.0005]
     grad. boosting  -0.1 points          [-0.9, +0.5]   log loss -0.0008 [-0.0016, +0.0001]
   This holds even though we only kept battles within 100 rating points,
   so even small skill gaps matter.

d) The model holds up on future battles.
   Logistic: 53.8% on val vs. 53.6% on test (the newest battles). Little
   is lost as the metagame shifts over time.

e) The probabilities are honest (calibrated), and modest.
   Sort the test battles into 10 groups by predicted P(p1 wins): the
   predicted and actual win rates match closely (e.g. 0.42 predicted vs.
   0.41 actual in the lowest group). The highest group is slightly
   overconfident: 0.58 predicted vs. 0.56 actual.
   The model rarely sounds confident, and it is right more often when it
   does (logistic, teams only):
     prediction within 47.5-52.5%:   42% of battles, right 51.7% of the time
     52.5-55% either way:            31% of battles, right 53.5%
     55-60% either way:              24% of battles, right 56.3%
     more than 60/40:                 3% of battles, right 61.1%
   Figure: figures/calibration_test.png.

f) Which information matters (ablation on val, gradient boosting).
   Removing type matchups (match_*) costs the most: +0.0024 log loss,
   -0.4 points accuracy. Next come the rating (+0.0016) and learned win
   rates (+0.0014), then types, base stats and species (+0.0008 to
   +0.0010). Every single drop is under half a point of accuracy, inside
   the noise. So no one feature group carries the model; the signal is spread
   thinly across all of them.

g) What the logistic weights show (results/logistic_top_coefficients.csv).
   - Biggest weights: learned pair win rates (+), "typical team" score
     (+), type-matchup effectiveness (+), and the number of Speed matchups
     won (+).
   - Overlapping features split credit and can flip sign:
     learned_species (-) vs. learned_pairs (+), and stats_spe (-) vs.
     match_outspeed (+). Read the group, not the sign of one weight.
   - Interesting pattern: the most-used Pokemon (Gholdengo, Dragapult,
     Iron Valiant, Gliscor, Hatterene) get NEGATIVE weights, and less
     common picks (Slitherwing, Pawmot, Klefki, Muk-Alola, Excadrill) get
     POSITIVE ones. One possible explanation (not tested): players who
     bring niche picks to 1300+ ladder may be more experienced, while
     "copied" meta teams are used by a wider range of players. That would
     be a player effect hiding inside the team features.

h) Some wrong predictions are probably not about teams at all.
   Example from the error analysis (data/interim/error_analysis/, kept
   private): a standard strong OU team (Raging Bolt, Ogerpon-Wellspring,
   Iron Treads, ...) lost to Komala / Mesprit / Spiritomb / Tauros /
   Zebstrika / Lapras. A result like that is more likely a disconnect,
   early forfeit or joke game than a real team matchup, which points to
   noise in the labels (see idea 1 below).


3. WHY THE CEILING IS LOW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

Team preview shows only the six species. It doesn't show moves, items,
abilities, EVs or Tera types, and it doesn't show the players' decisions
or luck (crits, misses, speed ties), which decide much of a battle. On top
of that, the 1300+ ladder already filters out most clearly bad teams, so
the teams we compare are fairly even. A modest edge is the expected
result, and measuring how big it is (about 3.5-4 points) is itself the
main finding.


4. IDEAS TO IMPROVE (best expected payoff first)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

IMPORTANT: the test set is now used up. Any change below must be tuned and
judged on VAL only. Report it as follow-up work, or score it on a NEW test
set: battles uploaded after 2026-10-05, which are not in our data yet.
Never re-run the current test set to pick a winner.

1. Clean the labels: drop forfeits and very short battles.  [likely biggest]
   Battles that end in a few turns (disconnects, rage-quits, joke teams)
   are noise; the teams didn't decide them. download.py only keeps the
   |player|, |poke| and |win| lines. Also keeping the |turn| lines (or the
   last turn number) would let parse_logs drop battles with fewer than
   ~5 turns. Cheap to do, and it fixes the data rather than the model.

2. Use all 533k clean battles instead of 100k.
   The learning curve is still slowly rising: about -0.0006 val log loss
   per doubling of training data. Estimate: +0.5 to 1 point of accuracy.
   Cost: set sample_size: null, everyone rebuilds, and the trees get much
   slower. Logistic regression would still train in minutes.

3. Player history features (for the teams + ratings setting).
   Rating alone is a crude skill measure, and point (g) hints that player
   effects are hiding in the species weights. Add, using ONLY battles
   before the current one: each player's number of past games, past win
   rate, and how often they've used this exact team. Team preview doesn't
   show this, but the ladder does, so it's fair for the "with player info"
   model.

4. Model the team as a whole.
   Our features are mostly single Pokemon, pairs and counts. Real team
   strength comes from combinations (weather: Pelipper + Swift Swim;
   hazard setters + removal; cores like Gholdengo + Great Tusk). Options:
   archetype flags (rain / sun / sand / hazard stack / hyper offense), or
   a small neural net that embeds each Pokemon and pools the team
   (a "Deep Sets" model; src/models/mlp.py is reserved for this).

5. Follow the metagame over time.
   Pokemon usage and counter-picks shift month to month. Weight recent
   training battles more, or compute learned win rates over a rolling
   window instead of all of train.

6. Small extras.
   - Turn the rating difference into Elo's expected score,
     1 / (1 + 10^(-diff/400)), or add rating x team interactions.
   - Calibrate the top of the range (e.g. Platt scaling on val), since the
     most confident 10% is ~2 points overconfident.

Not worth more time: more hyperparameter tuning (the val C curve is flat
between 0.0003 and 0.01), switching or ensembling these models (they're
tied, and averaging gave nothing), and removing feature groups (no group
matters much on its own).
